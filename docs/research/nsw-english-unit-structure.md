# NSW Department English unit structure (learned from a Stage 2 sample unit, 20 Oct 2026)

Source studied: NSW Department of Education, English Stage 2 Unit 1 (narrative, a novel study), 2023.
Licence stated in the document: Department-owned material is CC BY 4.0 (share and adapt, even commercially, with attribution "© State of New South Wales (Department of Education), 2023").
Not covered by that licence: Department logos, third-party material, NESA syllabus text (Crown copyright, NESA terms), ACARA progression text, and anything quoted from the novel.
Rule for us: we learn the structure and approach and write everything ourselves. We never reproduce the novel's text or illustrations in a product. Teachers use their own copy of the book (page references only).

## Shape of a unit
- 5 weeks, about 60 minutes a day, 4 days a week, in two connected parts:
  - Component A: foundational skills practised often (reading fluency, spelling, vocabulary and morphology, handwriting, sentence-level writing). Given as a teaching guide (focus area, teaching notes, examples drawn from the text) plus a planning scaffold; teachers plan the daily activities.
  - Component B: about 20 numbered lessons built around one mentor text and one textual concept (here narrative, with characterisation as the supporting concept).
- Front matter: overview, numbered teacher notes, an outcomes table with content points ticked by component and week, each point tagged with a National Literacy Learning Progression code, then a resource list.
- Each week of Component B opens with one learning intention and 4 to 6 success criteria ("Students can..."), and says success criteria are best co-constructed.
- Lessons are numbered activity lists, mostly teacher moves: revise, explain, model, think aloud, students do, share, reflect.
- Differentiation is two lines under the main task: "Too hard?" and "Too easy?" (13 times in this unit). This maps directly to our Get started / Main task / Go further.
- Assessment tasks are marked inside lessons (6 here) and state the outcome and content points the work sample shows.
- Lessons end with reflection questions and an exit ticket.
- Resources at the back (13 here): passage analysis tables for fluency and close reading, vocabulary and morphology examples, interaction protocols, graphic organisers (orientation mind map, story map, a narrative arc and its planning template), word banks (character traits), a Somebody-Wanted-But-So-Then summary frame.

## Teaching moves worth building into the engine
- Slow reveal: show one or two sentences before the cover and ask prediction questions about purpose, setting, problem and character.
- Read without showing illustrations first, students visualise and draw from the words, then compare with the illustrator's version.
- Mentor sentence or paragraph, analyse its structure, then innovate: students write their own version of the same pattern (for example a new character written in the same paragraph structure).
- Teacher models a piece of writing live; the model text is original, written by the teacher.
- Co-constructed success criteria for each writing task; peer feedback with a named protocol (Tell, Ask, Give); revise after feedback.
- Anchor charts built with the class and revisited in later lessons.
- Interaction protocols set up in lesson 1 and revisited.
- Turn and talk, Think-Pair-Share, mini whiteboards.
- Explicit grammar tied to the text: declarative sentences, adjective types (quantifying, descriptive, classifying), commas between adjectives, adverbial phrases.
- Morphology and syllabification of words from the passage (base + suffix, syllable counts).
- The unit ends with plan, draft (orientation, complication and events, resolution), revise and edit across the last week.

## What an English product from the engine needs (gaps to build)
- Schema: weeks, Component A guide, mentor text (title, author, chapters and page references only), textual concept, weekly LI and SC, lessons with activity steps, Too hard / Too easy, assessment task markers, resources.
- Slide types: slow reveal (prediction questions, text to be read aloud from the teacher's copy), anchor chart (built live, editable), mentor sentence analysis with colour-coded features using our own example sentences, model writing (our original text, in the handwriting font), co-construct success criteria, peer feedback protocol, reflection.
- Printables: graphic organisers (orientation map, story map, narrative arc planner, character traits bank, SWBST frame, adjective sort), writing paper with checklists, passage analysis tables (teacher fills from their copy), vocabulary and morphology cards.
- Images: no book covers or illustrations. Use our own icons, real photos of settings (for example a farm, a hillside, a tunnel), and drawn graphic organisers.
- Listing must say "You need a copy of the book."
